# Material list

What was gathered for the symbology repository, by topic. Every file named below is in one of two repositories: `symbology` (this one, public) or `symbology-restricted` (private, same layout under `material/`). A path in the tables is the absolute checkout path; the repository is in the path. Each file opens with a header block (title, author, edition, translator, year, source URL, date fetched, licence and its basis, text status). Where copyright was unclear the file is in the restricted repository and says so. Each topic ends with what could not be had, and why.

Open decisions for the living:
- Four GRETIL files in `material/sanskrit/jyotisha/` are public under GRETIL's CC BY-NC-SA although their base edition is modern (Bṛhatsaṃhitā after Tripathi 1968, Yogayātrā after Pingree 1972 and Jha 1986, Vivāhapaṭala after Lamsala 1978); a stricter reading moves them to restricted.
- Kramrisch 1928 (Viṣṇudharmottara part III) is US public domain by age but in copyright in India and the UK, so it is in the restricted repository.
- The public repository holds about 283 MB of Wallis scan PDFs under `material/ptolemy/harmonics/` (four files over GitHub's 50 MB advisory size, none over 100 MB).

Topics: Crowley; Ptolemy; Hesiod and the Homeric Hymns; the Orphic texts; Rudhyar, Jones and Ruperti; Sanskrit jyotisha; Sanskrit Purāṇa and śilpa; Sanskrit yoga, Tantra and medicine.

## Crowley: gathered works (topic: crowley)

Fetched 2026-11-21. Public repository root: /git/github.com/LiGoldragon/symbology/material/crowley/ . Restricted repository root: /git/github.com/LiGoldragon/symbology-restricted/material/crowley/ . No git command was run. Every file opens with a header block (title, author, edition, year, source URL, date fetched, licence and its basis, text status).

Copyright reasoning used throughout. Crowley died 1947, so his own words are public domain in the UK since 1 January 2018 (life plus 70). Lady Frieda Harris died 1962, so her paintings are in UK copyright to the end of 2032. In the US, works first published before 1930 are public domain by age; works first published in the UK in 1944 and 1955 most likely had their US copyright restored by the URAA (95 years from publication, to 2039 and 2050), and Ordo Templi Orientis claims the electronic editions. Hence the 1944 and 1955 books, all electronic editions that carry an OTO notice, the Weiser 1973 book and the card images are restricted ("copyright unclear"). Only a Wikisource text of a 1911 work is public.

Fetched 2026-11-21. Public repository root: /git/github.com/LiGoldragon/symbology/material/ptolemy/ . Restricted repository root: /git/github.com/LiGoldragon/symbology-restricted/material/ptolemy/ . No file passes 90 MB (largest: 76,375,620 bytes).

Copyright reasoning used. Ptolemy's own words are public domain; what decides is the edition, the translation and the digitisation. Editions, translations and prints from before 1930 by authors long dead are public (US public domain by age; the old editors died long ago). The standing rule of the earlier runs is followed: copyright unclear goes to restricted. Under copyright, and openly downloadable only as unauthorised uploads on archive.org (Solomon 1999, Barker 1989, Toomer 1984, Smith 1996), they are restricted. Düring 1930 is restricted too: Düring died 1984 (life plus 70 runs to 2054 in Sweden and the EU), the file is the Olms 1982 reprint and a shadow-library upload. Note on the 2026 date: works first published in 1930 became US public domain on 1 January 2026, so the 1930 Düring first printing is public in the US, but the file is the 1982 reprint, so it stays restricted.

## Could not be had, and why

- Greek of the Harmonics as clean public text: none exists openly. Düring 1930 is had only as the restricted Olms reprint. Wallis's Greek (1682, 1699) is kept as page scans because every OCR garbles it; a Tesseract grc+lat trial of this run on a 1682 page returned half-garbled Greek, so no re-OCR is claimed. The sharper Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève scan of Wallis 1682 (https://archive.org/details/BSG_4V589INV1360RES) is 891 MB and was not kept; its OCR text is unusable.
- Porphyry's Commentary on the Harmonics: only Wallis 1699 (Greek and Latin, first edition) and a short fragment in Gogava 1562. Düring's 1932 Greek edition (Göteborg) and Barker's Porphyry's Commentary on Ptolemy's Harmonics (Cambridge 2015, paperback 2024) were not found on archive.org or elsewhere openly; Barker 2000 Scientific Method in Ptolemy's Harmonics (https://archive.org/details/scientificmethod0000bark) is lending only and was not taken. Barker 1989 (restricted) holds extracts of Porphyry.
- Older English translations of the Harmonics: none exists in the public domain; the first English translation is Barker 1989 (extracts), then Solomon 1999, both restricted above.
- Beinecke MS 208 and MS 270 (Ptolemy Harmonics and Porphyry commentary): the archive items hold only a few lines of catalogue text, no manuscript text; not taken.
- Almagest English: Taliaferro 1952 (https://archive.org/details/almagestonrevolu0000ptol) is lending only on archive.org (access-restricted) and was not taken; Toomer 1984 is had as an unauthorised upload (restricted). No public domain English Almagest exists beyond the 1952 and 1984 books.

## Could not be had, and why

- Proclus' commentary on the Works and Days and the Byzantine scholia in English translation: none openly published found; the Greek is in the scholia files.
- Tzetzes' scholia on the Works and Days stand inside the Gaisford volume (a heading "ΤΟΥ ΤΖΕΤΖΟΥ" is in it); no separate Tzetzes file. Moschopulus' scholia were not located by heading in the OCR and are not claimed.
- The Plutarch commentary on Hesiod, as a separate modern text (Sandbach's Loeb Plutarch Moralia XV, 1969, and Bernardakis vol. 7, 1896 scan, were not read): copyright for Sandbach; the Didot 1855 fragments are what is gathered. - Modern translations (Lattimore, West, Athanassakis, Most Loeb, Wender): in copyright, not gathered; the brief asked for an English only where public domain, and five public-domain Hesiod English translations and five Hymn translations are held.
- A clean keyboarded Greek of the Homeric Hymns in a second edition: the Perseus text follows Evelyn-White's Loeb; Allen-Sikes 1904 is OCR only; Allen-Halliday-Sikes 1936 is in copyright.

## Could not be had, and why

- Quandt, Orphei Hymni (Berlin: Weidmann, 1941; 3rd ed. 1955): only a lending copy on archive.org (orpheihymni0000guil, text download refused, 401); the 1941 text is presumed in copyright (the editor died after 1950), and no open copy was found. The readable Greek of the Hymns is Abel 1885 (the Greek Wikisource transcription) and Hermann 1805; Quandt's critical text is the gap.
- Athanassakis 1977 (Scholars Press, Orphic Hymns: Text, Translation and Notes): lending copy only (isbn_9780891301196, 401). The 2013 revised edition by Athanassakis and Wolkow was openly downloadable and is in the restricted repository.
- Derveni papyrus: the Greek text of the 2006 edition (CHS web page) is in the restricted repository, flattened from HTML, much of it sigla and lacunae. No translation was openly available: Betegh 2004 (Cambridge), Janko's translation and the Laks-Most 1997 volume are paywalled or lending only; the ZPE PDFs of Janko's interim text (2002) and Janko 2008 could not be reached (the uni-koeln.de download paths returned 404). Franco Ferrari's new edition of columns I to VI at CHS is not fetched.
- The Orphic Argonautica and the Lithica in English: no public-domain English translation found; a modern translation (Colavito 2011) is, as far as I know, under copyright and was not looked for. Greek (Wikisource el, Gesner 1764, Abel 1881) is had. The Lithica has no clean text edition, only OCR with weak Greek and a PDF scan.
- Hermann 1805 (a Google Books scan catalogued as Orphei Argonautica, bub_gb_S5nlPpxzhDYC) and a second scan of Diehl 1903 (proclidiadochii00diehgoog) were fetched and discarded: the OCR of the Greek is unreadable (Cyrillic or Latin-letter noise).
- Guthrie 1935 first edition: only the 1993 Princeton paperback of the second edition was had; the open upload is restricted for copyright.
- Proclus, Platonic Theology: Taylor 1816 (English) and the Budé Greek volumes I, V and VI only; the Budé volumes II to IV (Books II to IV) are not on archive.org; Portus's 1618 Greek edition was not found. Proclus's Timaeus commentary Greek: Diehl 1903 volume 1 only; volumes 2 and 3 (1904, 1906) were not found as separate scans. Proclus In Parmenidem (Cousin) and the Cratylus are partial; Taylor's Euclid commentaries (1792) were left out as off topic.
- Damascius: Ruelle's 1889 Greek edition (Paris: Klincksieck) was not found as a scan; only Kopp 1826 and Ruelle 1861. No public-domain English translation (Ahbel-Rappe 2010 is restricted).
- Kern's index and Greek words: the Kern 1922 OCR text and the PDF are as the library scanned them; no corrected digital text of the Orphic fragments was found in the open.
- sacred-texts.com (Taylor's hymns and Mead's Orpheus as text) refused automated access (Cloudflare, 403); Theoi and archive.org were used instead.
- Linforth, The Arts of Orpheus (1941), openly downloadable (artsoforpheus00linf) but not asked for; not fetched. A separate item The Orphic Pantheon by G. R. S. Mead (the-orphic-pantheon-by-g-r-s-mead, 1896, 95 KB) was not fetched or compared with the Orpheus book.

## Could not be had, and why

Sanskrit and commentary
- Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, clean Sanskrit from GRETIL or Muktabodha: GRETIL has no BPHS; the Muktabodha host (muktalib5.org) gave no connection from the fetcher, and Muktabodha asks for registration in any case; sanskritdocuments.org answered with an automated-access block. The Sanskrit taken is the enjoylearningsanskrit.com text (restricted, site "all rights reserved"). The Sanskrit with traditional commentary is the OCR of the 1923 Subodhinī (public) and the Sudhā of Dev Chandra Jha (restricted); the Telugu-script Sanskrit BPHS (archive.org brihat-parashara-hora-shastra) was not taken, and Santhanam's Devanagari verses were lost to the OCR.
- Wikisource (sa.wikisource.org): the API answered every request from the fetcher with a Wikimedia error page, so the Wikisource Bṛhajjātaka and Bṛhatsaṃhitā pages were not taken; GRETIL gives both Sanskrit texts.
- Phaladīpikā, Jātakapārijāta, Sārāvalī: no Sanskrit e-text from GRETIL or Muktabodha. Sanskrit exists only inside scans: the Sanskrit lines of V. Subrahmanya Sastri's volumes were recognised as Latin letters and are lost in the OCR (the English is clean); the 1907 Nirnaya Sagar Sārāvalī Sanskrit is taken; the 1928 third edition (archive.org dli.csl.7888) and the 1922 Jātakapārijāta Sanskrit (in.ernet.dli.2015.405738) were left out because the OCR is unusable. The Hindi-commentary Jātakapārijāta (Kashi Sanskrit Series 10) is taken, restricted. Not taken though available as scans: the Phaladīpikā with Hindi by Hari Shankar Pathak and by Gopesh Kumar Ojha, the Kāśī Sanskrit Series Sārāvalī commentaries, the Adhikaraṇa-sārāvalī (a different work).
- Traditional commentary on the Bṛhajjātaka: the Nauka ṭīkā (public) and Sitaram Jha's Bhaṭṭotpala Vivṛtti (restricted) are taken, both with noisy Devanagari OCR; the 1864 and 1852 Bhaṭṭotpala lithographs were left out as unreadable OCR; the Vimalā commentary of Achyutanand Jha (1957) has only 32 KB of text in the archive.org item. For the Bṛhatsaṃhitā only Part 1 of Utpala's commentary (Vizianagram Sanskrit Series 18, 1895) and the 1880 Calcutta edition with Utpala are taken; the Tripathi 1968 and Pandeya 2002/2008 Sampurnanand editions with Hindi were not taken (in copyright).

Translations
- Kern's Bṛhatsaṃhitā translation (1870-1875): taken from the four JRAS volumes in six instalments, each ending "To be continued"; the last instalment in vol. 7 (1875) starts at chapter LXV; whether further instalments exist in later volumes was not checked, so the translation may stop short of the whole work. The complete English translation is Bhat 1981/1986 (restricted, Part 1 clean, Part 2 English clean) and Iyer 1884 (public).
- Santhanam's Sārāvalī: only chapters 1-10 as an English text export (restricted); the full two-volume archive.org item (saravaliofkalyanavarmasanthanamr) was recognised in the wrong script and left out; a PDF text layer was not tried. Santhanam's BPHS vols. 1 and 2 are taken (restricted), but his Sanskrit verses are lost to the OCR.
- Sastri and Bhat's two-volume Bṛhatsaṃhitā 1947 (archive.org brihatsamhitavarahamihirabruhatsubrahmanyasastriv...) is OCR in the wrong script; the 1946 upload (1.36 MB of text) is taken instead and was not compared for completeness.
- Iyer's Bṛhatsaṃhitā 1885 with appendix, B. Suryanarain Rao's 1919 Bṛhajjātaka, the dli second edition of Sastri's Bṛhajjātaka, and Girish Chand Sharma's BPHS translation: archive.org OCR in the wrong script, left out (Iyer 1884, Iyer 1885 and 1905, Raman-Rao 1957 and the other Sastri upload were taken instead).
- Pingree's Yavanajātaka: both volumes are taken (restricted). Volume 1 (edition and translation) is an OCR made here from the PDF, because archive.org's own text of it is in the wrong script; its Devanagari is recognised poorly; volume 2 is the PDF text layer. Mīnarāja's Vṛddhayavanajātaka as a separate open text was not found. The archive.org lending copy (yavanajatakaofsp0000sphu) cannot be downloaded.
- Kapoor's Phaladīpikā is taken as a PDF text layer from panchanga.lv (restricted). Sastri's Phaladīpikā of 1937 and 1950 are taken (restricted).
- Sūryasiddhānta: Burgess 1860 (public, the JAOS issue), Bāpū Deva Śāstrī 1861 (public), Hall 1859 with Raṅganātha's exposition (public) and GRETIL's Sanskrit are taken; the Burgess text in the 1935 Gangooly reprint is kept in restricted for its modern notes. The archive.org item "Surya Siddhanta Ebenezer Burgess 1860" (suryasiddhantaebenzerburgess1860_588_E) was recognised in the wrong script and left out.
- Kern's 1865 Sanskrit edition: the archive.org text (Bibliotheca Indica) is poorly recognised Devanagari; a second archive.org item of it (wg1077) was left out as worse. GRETIL gives the clean Sanskrit.

Garga and Parāśara pieces
- Garga: the Vṛddhagargasaṃhitā or the jyotiṣa Gargasaṃhitā as a whole was not found in any open source. The nearest is Mitchiner's critical edition and translation of the Yuga Purāṇa with his introduction on Garga and Utpala's citations (restricted, 1986). The "Garga Saṃhitā" items on archive.org (Gita Press and others) are the Kṛṣṇa-centred Purāṇic work and were not taken; Utpala's quotations of Garga are inside the Bṛhatsaṃhitā commentary files.
- Parāśara: the Parāśara Tantra in R. N. Iyengar's 2013 reconstruction (restricted) and the Laghu and Madhya Pārāśarī (public, 1916 and 1926 scans) are taken; Kṛṣi-Parāśara is agriculture and was not taken.

Also available at GRETIL and not taken (outside this brief): Lagadha's Vedāṅgajyotiṣa, the Āryabhaṭīya with commentary, Brahmagupta's Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta.

Text status in short. Most English and Sanskrit files are the raw OCR text layer of Internet Archive scans (marked "-ocr" in the name; recognition errors left, only runs of spaces and blank lines collapsed). Copyright reasoning used throughout. Public means public domain in the United States by age (first published before 1931) with the author dead long ago, or a transcription of such an edition. ## Could not be had, and why

- Bṛhatsaṃhitā building chapters: not gathered here; taken whole by the jyotisha gatherer (see above), so nothing is missing.
- A clean text of the Taluqdar English Matsyapurana (chapters 93-94 among them): only the OCR of the Sacred Books of the Hindus scan exists (words such as "planetarv oflermgs" show the recognition level); no Wikisource, Wisdomlib or Gutenberg text of that translation was found (the Wisdomlib address tried for the Matsya Purana returned 404, and vedapath.app, which the earlier research found closed with HTTP 403, was not tried again). The Sanskrit of ch. 93-94 is clean in the Wikisource and GRETIL files.
- Yajnavalkyasmriti: Vidyarnava's English (with the translation of the Mitaksara) is only Book I, the Achara adhyaya, which holds 1.293-308; Books II and III (Vyavahara, Prayascitta) were not taken. The Sanskrit with the Mitaksara is the Nirnayasagara edition of 1892 (OCR); the Wikisource page of a 1936 scan with commentary is an index of page images only and gave no text; the GRETIL text is verses only (restricted). Other editions on archive.org (Ganapati Sastri's Trivandrum edition, 1912, 7 MB OCR; further Gharpure volumes of 1936-41) were seen and not taken.
- Garudapurana: no traditional commentary exists for the Purana as such; the Saroddhara recension with a tika (1888) is gathered. A Wikisource transcription of the Garudapurana exists (323 pages) but its fetch was too slow behind the shared request limit and was stopped after 11 pages; the GRETIL text (restricted, edition undated) and the 1890 Calcutta edition (OCR) stand in its place. Dutt's translation: the scan of the Google copy is complete; a DLI three-volume copy of the same work was not kept.
- Agnipurana: Dutt's four DLI volumes overlap and carry the 1987 Cosmo reprint covers; chapter continuity between the volumes was checked only by a heading scan. No Sanskrit-with-commentary edition (there is none) and no further English (Shastri and Bhatt's later parts) beyond Gangadharan.
- Vishnudharmottarapurana Part III: Kramrisch's book prints only the translation (no Sanskrit); the Sanskrit is the Wikisource transcription (clean, base edition not named, 347 of 355 chapter markers found by script) and the DLI Venkateshwara Press scan (OCR, poor). Priyabala Shah's critical edition (Gaekwad's Oriental Series 130, 1958, chapters 1-118) is gathered as OCR; her volume II (GOS 137, 1961, introduction and summary translation) was not found as text. Her Ahmedabad English translation of Khanda III is gathered (chapter coverage not checked). The 1928 first edition of Kramrisch's book (with notes) was not taken.
- Mayamata: Dagens's French Part 2 (chapters XXVI-XXXVI, 1976) was not found; the French Part 1 (1970, OCR) is gathered. Dagens's 1994 English translation and critical text (both volumes) exist only as re-read OCR made here; a second Internet Archive scan of Volume II (Motilal 1997, 286 MB) was not taken as it repeats the same volume. No traditional commentary on the Mayamata is known.
- Manasara: Acharya's Sanskrit text and English translation are the 1933 and 1934 Oxford volumes (the Internet Archive scans); the Sanskrit is Devanagari OCR with the English notes mixed in, the translation the Munshiram reprint of 1994. The other Manasara Series volumes (II, V, VI, VII) were not taken except the Dictionary (Vol. I). No traditional commentary exists. The Sanskrit scan PDF is 366 MB and was not kept.
- Natyasastra: Ghosh's translation is Vol. I only (chapters I-XXVII, which holds chapters 6 and 23); Vol. II (chapters XXVIII-XXXVI, the music chapters, 1961) was not taken. The Sanskrit is the Wikisource transcription of all 37 chapters (clean, base edition not named) plus Ghosh's own text volumes and the four Gaekwad's Oriental Series volumes with Abhinavagupta's Abhinavabharati, all as OCR. GRETIL's partial text lacks chapters 17, 31, 32, 34 and names no print source. The series numbers of the GOS volumes in the headers are from memory.
- Silparatna: no English or other translation exists in anything found; only the Sanskrit of Trivandrum Sanskrit Series 75 (1922) and 98 (1929), as OCR of moderate quality, with the scan PDFs kept beside it because no better text exists. The painting chapter (chitralakshana, ch. 46) is in Part 1.
- Gopinatha Rao: the OCR carries the text and the appendices; the plates and line drawings are not in the text files. Vol. I Part I was taken from the Asiatic Society of Bombay copy (the other scans of that part differ in recognition quality), Vol. I Part II from the Lucknow copy.
- Kramrisch, The Hindu Temple (1946): the two volumes are OCR text only; the photographs and the plates are not in it. The 1976 Motilal reissue was not taken.
- Not attempted (named in the earlier research, not in this order): Vaikhanasasmartasutra, Aparajitaprccha, Samarangana-sutradhara, Manasollasa, Rupamandana, Silparatnakosa (IGNCA).

## Rules used

- Public: the work is ancient and the file is an edition or translation first published before 1930 (US public domain by age), or a transcription (Wikisource, GRETIL) of an ancient text that cites no modern edition. Wikisource transcriptions carry their CC BY-SA 4.0 licence, GRETIL files their CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; each header says so.
- Restricted, "copyright unclear": a GRETIL file that transcribes a named modern edition (1932 onward: Thomi 1993, Chaukhambha 1970 and 1994, Motilal 1964); a book first published in India or elsewhere after 1929 whose author or editor died after 1945 or is unknown (Tantrik Texts 16 of 1933, Jha 1942, Gulabkunverba 1949, Poona 1933, Adyar 1933, Khemraj and others); a book under a copyright notice (Rai 1980, Sharma 1981, Sharma and Dash 2001, Khemraj 1994).
- An archive.org "CC0" or "Public Domain Mark" label on a scan of an Indian library (Digital Library of India, eGangotri) is the library's label on its scan and is not relied on; the basis for each public file is its publication date.
- "OCR" files are the archive.org full text of a scan, uncorrected. Latin script (English) is clean enough to read; Devanagari recognition varies from good to poor (stated in each file's text_status and below). Wikisource files have had the `{{header}}` block, `<poem>` tags and `<br>` removed and keep a `<!-- wikisource page: ... -->` comment before each page. GRETIL files are IAST text with their own header kept.
- Where the Sanskrit and the translation are in separate files they are siblings in the same directory. No traditional Sanskrit commentary exists for the Gheraṇḍasaṃhitā or the Śivasaṃhitā; their only old commentaries are Hindi bhāṣā glosses (Gheraṇḍa, Śivasvarodaya), which are kept.

## Where the mahābhūta and guṇa passages are (for the flow that will use them)

- Gheraṇḍasaṃhitā III (five dhāraṇās, verses 3.70-80 in the editions the research opened; 3.59-63 in the Kaivalyadhama numbering): Sanskrit gheranda-samhita/sanskrit-wikisource.md (chapter 3), English english-vasu-1914-15-ssp-1979-reprint-ocr.md.
- Śivasvarodaya (tattvas by svara, shapes, colours): English rama-prasad-1894 (verses 141-207 as he numbers them); Rai's text and translation in the restricted repository.
- Ṣaṭcakranirūpaṇa verses 4-29 with Woodroffe's notes (tables of element, colour and maṇḍala shape): satcakranirupana/serpent-power-woodroffe-second-revised-edition-1924-ocr.md.
- Śāradātilaka I.19-24 with Rāghavabhaṭṭa's "bhūtamaṇḍalāny āha" commentary: Sanskrit in the restricted 1933 and Chowkhamba files (the commentary is not in the public Vidyāsāgara edition).
- Sāṃkhyakārikā 12, 13, 38 and Gauḍapāda and Vācaspati on them; Bhagavadgītā 14, 17, 18 (all eighteen chapters are in the files, Śaṅkara's bhāṣya to chapter 17 in GRETIL and to 18 in the 1910 OCR and in Mahadeva Sastri's English); Yogasūtra with Vyāsa; Caraka Sūtra 26 and Śārīra 1 (Wikisource Sanskrit; Kaviratna English), Suśruta Sūtra 41 (Wikisource Sanskrit; Bhishagratna vol. 1), Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya Sūtrasthāna (GRETIL); Taittirīya II.1 (ākāśād vāyuḥ) and Chāndogya VI (red, white and black) in the Sanskrit, Śaṅkara, Müller and other translations below.

## Could not be had, and why

- Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya, K. R. Srikantha Murthy's English translation (Krishnadas Academy, Varanasi, from 1991; fifth edition 2001): no text edition is open anywhere found. The Groningen Indian-medicine repository lists it without full text; the archive.org item "ashtangahridayavagbhattaenglishtranssrikanthamurthyk.r.vol1chowkambha" is garbled and was discarded. The Sanskrit of the Sūtrasthāna (GRETIL), of the other sthānas (Wikisource) and Aruṇadatta's Sarvāṅgasundarā on the whole work (Kunte 1880 and 1902, OCR) are gathered; no English translation of the Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya is in the repository.
- Śāradātilaka: no English translation of the whole is open (Bühnemann's chapter 25 is in a 1992 journal). Rāghavabhaṭṭa's Padārthādarśa is gathered only in the restricted 1933 and Chowkhamba editions (OCR, Devanagari partly poor); the public 1882 Vidyāsāgara edition has the Tantra without the commentary.
- Śivasvarodaya: a clean Devanagari or IAST e-text was not had (sanskritdocuments.org answers a Cloudflare challenge, and GRETIL and Wikisource do not hold it). The Sanskrit is in the 1919 Jagadishvar OCR (public, Hindi bhāṣā by Ramdhin Sharma) and in the restricted Rai and Khemraj files. Rama Prasad's English (1890, 1894) is the public translation. Rai's edition and translation (1980) is not openly available; it is in the restricted repository (three witnesses, OCR and the Ambuda proofing text, pages not fully proofread).
- Haṭhayogapradīpikā: the Jyotsnā is gathered in the Nirnaya Sagar 1882 edition (Wikisource clean Devanagari with the whole work, plus the OCR scan and 1893 and 1895 witnesses). The Adyar (Theosophical Publishing House, Madras) 1933 edition of the Jyotsnā is held only as a restricted scan. Pancham Sinh's 1914-15 translation (public) is the English; modern translations (Muktibodhananda 1998, Mallinson and others) are under copyright and were not taken.
- Gheraṇḍasaṃhitā: Mallinson's translation (used in the research file) is under copyright and was not taken; Vasu 1914-15 is the public English. Thomi's 1993 critical Sanskrit (GRETIL) is restricted. No Sanskrit commentary exists; only the two Kalyan Hindi glosses of 1913 and 1929.
- Śivasaṃhitā: the Sanskrit is Wikisource's clean text; Vasu's own Devanagari in the scan is unreadable, and Vasu's English is the OCR.
- Sāṃkhyakārikā: Gauḍapāda's bhāṣya is in four Sanskrit forms and in Wilson's 1837 English. Vācaspati's Tattvakaumudī is in Jha's English of 1896 (the book's Sanskrit text was not captured by the OCR) and in the 1917 Sanskrit OCR; a clean Sanskrit Tattvakaumudī e-text was not had. The 1892 Jīvānanda Vidyāsāgara scan of Gauḍapāda and one other archive scan of the Tattvakaumudī were garbled and discarded.
- Yogasūtra: Vācaspati's Tattvavaiśāradī is available in Woods's English and in the 1917 Bodas Sanskrit OCR (poor Devanagari). Rama Prasad's English of 1912 has no Devanagari in this OCR.
- Bhagavadgītā: Śaṅkara's bhāṣya in GRETIL ends at chapter 17; chapter 18 in Sanskrit is the 1910 OCR only. Gambhirananda's translation of Śaṅkara, Adidevananda's of Rāmānuja and other modern ones are under copyright and were not taken. Rāmānuja's English is Govindacharya 1898.
- Carakasaṃhitā: the Sanskrit with Cakrapāṇi is not available as clean text in full (Wikisource has seven of eight sthānas, its Cikitsāsthāna page is empty; GRETIL has parts and is restricted; the Gulabkunverba 1949 volumes are restricted and poorly recognised). Kaviratna's English (public) stops in the Cikitsāsthāna; P. V. Sharma 1981 (volume 1, Sūtra to Indriya) and one Sharma and Dash volume are restricted; the rest of Sharma and Dash and Sharma's later volumes were not found as text.
- Suśrutasaṃhitā: Dalhaṇa's commentary is present only in OCR (1885 part 1 and 1915 whole, poor-to-fair Devanagari); the Wikisource Sanskrit is clean without the commentary. Bhishagratna's three volumes are public; volume 2 is held twice (two scans).
- Taittirīya and Chāndogya: Śaṅkara's Sanskrit bhāṣya for the Taittirīya is in the 1910 Vani Vilas OCR (public) and the GRETIL file (restricted); the Wikisource Taittirīya bhāṣya page holds only a scan index. Chāndogya Śaṅkara's Sanskrit is in Wikisource (clean) and in GRETIL (Sansknet). Jha's revised 1942 English is restricted; his 1899 text is public.
- Serpent Power: the first edition (Luzac, London, 1919) was not found as a text; the second revised edition (Ganesh, 1924) is the public witness, with the later (third, fourth) printings of 1950 onward not taken. Pūrṇānanda's text and the Kālīcaraṇa commentary in Sanskrit are in the 1913 Tantrik Texts OCR (Devanagari fair).
- The Mallinson, Rai and Muktibodhananda translations, and the modern critical editions, are the main under-copyright works not taken or taken only as restricted files.
